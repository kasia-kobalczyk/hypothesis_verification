You are diagnosing why a literature search did or did not produce useful evidence
for a scientific proposition. This is a post-mortem on a retrieval system, not a
scientific review.

A verification system generated the proposition below, wrote search queries for it,
retrieved papers published before a fixed cutoff date, and asked a model whether
those papers bear on the proposition. You are being shown all of it. Decide WHERE
the process succeeded or broke down.

THE PROPOSITION:
$proposition

CUTOFF DATE (nothing published after this date was retrievable):
$cutoff

QUERIES THE SYSTEM WROTE:
$queries

PAPERS IT RETRIEVED AND SHOWED TO THE ASSESSOR ($n_papers shown):
$papers

WHAT THE ASSESSOR CONCLUDED:
label: $evidence_label
rationale: $rationale

Choose exactly one attribution:

- "informative_evidence_found": the retrieved literature genuinely bears on the
  proposition and the assessor used it. The process worked.

- "no_relevant_historical_evidence_exists": the queries were reasonable and the
  retrieved papers are the sort of thing that would have surfaced the evidence if
  it existed, but the pre-cutoff literature simply does not address this. Nobody
  had done the study yet. This is a fact about the state of science at the cutoff,
  NOT a failure of the system -- and it is not evidence against the proposition.

- "retrieval_failure": relevant pre-cutoff work plausibly exists, but the queries
  or the search missed it. Symptoms: queries too narrow, too broad, using the
  wrong vocabulary for the field, or naming a specific construct when the
  literature uses another term.

- "evidence_assessor_failure": the retrieved papers DO bear on the proposition,
  but the assessor said otherwise -- it missed a relevant paper it was shown, or
  read one wrongly.

- "generic_compatibility_only": the papers are topically related and the assessor
  treated them as support, but they do not actually test the proposition. They
  establish that the general area is real, which any competing explanation would
  also predict. This is the failure mode where a system looks well-evidenced and
  has learned nothing.

- "construct_mismatch": the proposition is not the kind of thing this literature
  could confirm or deny as written. It names a measurement, population, timescale
  or condition that does not correspond to how the field actually reports results,
  so no retrievable paper could match it even in principle.

Two rules that override your instinct:

1. An empty or unhelpful retrieval is NEVER evidence that the proposition is
   false. If you find yourself reasoning that the absence of papers counts
   against it, you have made an error -- choose between
   "no_relevant_historical_evidence_exists" and "retrieval_failure" instead.

2. Distinguish "no relevant evidence exists" from "retrieval failed" on the
   evidence of the QUERIES, not on the outcome. Ask: would a competent
   researcher, searching this literature before the cutoff with these terms, have
   expected to find something? If yes and nothing came back, that is retrieval
   failure.

Return a single JSON object and nothing else:

{
  "attribution": "<one of the six names above>",
  "reasoning": "<two or three sentences>",
  "would_a_better_query_have_helped": <true|false|null>,
  "suggested_query": "<only if retrieval_failure, else null>",
  "confidence": "<high|medium|low>"
}
