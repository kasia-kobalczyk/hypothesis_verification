You are auditing a scientific reasoning system after the fact.

The system was given a phenomenon and two competing explanations, and asked what
else should be true if each explanation held. It generated the proposition below.
Your job is to classify that proposition. You are NOT judging whether the system
picked the right explanation, and you are NOT being asked what is true.

$phenomenon

COMPETING EXPLANATIONS:
$hypotheses

REFERENCE DISCRIMINATORS.
These are the observable differences that the later resolving science actually
turned on. They were hidden from the system when it generated its proposition;
they are shown to you only so you can recognise a match. A proposition counts as
recovering one if it targets the SAME observable contrast, even in different
words, at a different level of detail, or with the direction stated the other way
round. It does not count merely because it concerns the same topic.

$reference_discriminators

THE GENERATED PROPOSITION:
$proposition

STEP 1 — Predict the proposition's status under EACH explanation, separately.

For each explanation, say what that explanation ON ITS OWN implies about the
proposition. Use exactly one of:
- "positive_or_present"   : the explanation implies this should hold / be observed
- "negative_or_absent"    : the explanation implies this should NOT hold
- "neutral_or_no_change"  : the explanation implies this specific quantity is
                            unchanged -- an actual null prediction, positively made
- "indeterminate"         : the explanation simply does not speak to this. It makes
                            no commitment either way.

The distinction between "neutral_or_no_change" and "indeterminate" is the single
most important judgment you make here, so make it deliberately.

"neutral_or_no_change" means the explanation genuinely predicts no effect: it says
the quantity stays put. "indeterminate" means the explanation is silent -- it is
compatible with the proposition being true and also with it being false.

An explanation being silent is NOT a prediction of absence. If you find yourself
reasoning "explanation B doesn't mention this, so under B it wouldn't happen",
stop: that is "indeterminate", not "negative_or_absent". Silence is the most
common way an audit like this manufactures a contrast that was never there.

STEP 2 — Is the proposition genuinely discriminative?

It is discriminative only if the two explanations yield DIFFERENT statuses AND
neither status is "indeterminate". A contrast between a prediction and a silence
is not a contrast.

STEP 3 — Assign exactly one category.

- "reference_discriminator_recovered": discriminative, and it targets the same
  observable contrast as one of the reference discriminators above. Name which.

- "novel_plausible_discriminator": discriminative and scientifically sound, but not
  among the reference discriminators. This is a success, not a failure -- the
  reference list is what the resolving study happened to use, not an exhaustive
  list of what could have been used.

- "compatible_non_discriminative": a real, checkable consequence, but both
  explanations predict the same status. True, relevant, and no help in choosing.

- "generic_component_fact": a background fact about the system, mechanism or
  method that would be affirmed by the field regardless of which explanation holds.
  It is assessable against literature -- that is what makes it tempting -- but it
  carries no information about the dispute.

- "invalid_or_unsupported": not a consequence of either explanation, scientifically
  garbled, not empirically checkable as stated, or a restatement of the phenomenon.

- "silence_as_null_error": it LOOKS discriminative only because one explanation is
  silent about it and that silence has been read as a prediction of absence or of
  no change. Use this when your Step 1 answer for one explanation is
  "indeterminate" but the proposition is framed as though that explanation
  positively predicts the opposite.
