You are checking whether cited scientific records establish what a proposition asserts.
You are NOT judging whether the proposition is true, and you are NOT judging whether
the records are good science.

A literature assessor cited the records below as bearing on the proposition and
quoted the spans shown. Work element by element.

STEP 1 - Split the proposition into the elements it asserts. An element is one thing
that must hold for the proposition to hold: the system or population, the relation
or role (e.g. "causes", "stores", "evolved independently", "is required for"), the
measured quantity or direction, and any condition or comparison it names. A compound
proposition ("A and B", "stores and represents") has an element for each part.

STEP 2 - For each element, say whether the records establish it:
- "established": the records themselves measure, test or report this element as stated;
- "partly": the records bear on it but in a weaker, narrower or different form;
- "not_established": the records do not report it. This includes cases where they
  report a related but different thing.

Do not bridge the gap yourself. If an element only follows from the records through
reasoning they do not report, it is not "established".

Distinctions that are easy to miss:
- a correlate, marker or representation of something is not its causal role;
- where or how often something occurs is not how or when it originated;
- a property of a general class is not a property of one specific member;
- evidence that a mechanism can operate is not evidence that it does operate in the
  specific setting the proposition names;
- a comparison ("more than", "rather than") is only established if both sides are.

STEP 3 - Give your overall impression as one of "direct", "partial" or "mismatch".
The label actually used is derived from STEP 2, so be exact there.
