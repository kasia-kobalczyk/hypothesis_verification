Decide what one claim predicts about one measurement. Follow the steps in order.

STEP 1. Read the claim. State what it asserts: which entities, which relation
between them, under what conditions.

STEP 2. Read the measurement. State exactly what quantity is measured, in what
system, what is varied, and what the comparison is against.

STEP 3. Does the claim bear on that quantity, under those conditions?
        If NO -> the answer is "claim_does_not_determine". Stop.
        If YES -> go to step 4.

STEP 4. Assume the claim is TRUE. Relative to the stated comparator, does it imply
        the quantity goes UP or APPEARS, goes DOWN or is ABSENT, or stays
        APPROXIMATELY UNCHANGED?

THE DISTINCTION THAT MATTERS MOST HERE

  "approximately unchanged" is a SUBSTANTIVE PREDICTION. It means the claim, if
  true, implies the quantity sits at baseline -- that the effect is absent. It is a
  real, falsifiable prediction: an experiment could refute it by finding an effect.

  "claim does not determine" means something completely different. It means the
  claim is SILENT about this quantity -- it licenses no prediction at all, neither
  directional nor null.

  Never answer "approximately unchanged" merely because the claim says nothing.
  Never answer "claim does not determine" when the claim actually implies the
  effect is absent.

Two contrasting examples:

  Claim:   "Protein X is not involved in pathway Y."
  Test:    knock down X, measure pathway Y activity.
  Answer:  approximately_unchanged
           -- the claim positively implies no effect. It is falsifiable here.

  Claim:   "Protein X affects mitochondrial morphology."
  Test:    knock down X, measure pathway Y activity.
  Answer:  claim_does_not_determine
           -- the claim is about morphology and says nothing about pathway Y.
