You are identifying the part of a scientific proposition that could tell candidate
explanations apart. You are NOT judging whether the proposition is true.

CANDIDATE EXPLANATIONS:
$candidates

WHAT EACH CANDIDATE WAS JUDGED TO PREDICT ABOUT THE PROPOSITION:
$states

PROPOSITION:
$proposition

Decompose the proposition into:

- shared_context: background that every candidate accepts or that holds whichever candidate
  is correct (the system, the general setting, things no candidate disputes);
- contrast_variable: the specific variable, ordering, state or relation whose value the
  candidates disagree about. This is the piece that makes the proposition discriminative;
- contrast_direction_or_state: the value of that variable which the proposition asserts;
- system_or_population: where it is asserted to hold;
- measurement_or_observable: what one would measure or observe to establish the
  contrast_variable's value.

If no part of the proposition carries a disagreement between the candidates, set
"has_contrast" to false and give the proposition's own central asserted variable as
contrast_variable.

Keep each field short and concrete. Do not add claims the proposition does not make.

Illustration (a dispute that is not part of this task). Candidates about why Lake X began
blooming: nutrient runoff vs rising temperature. Proposition: "In Lake X, bloom onset
followed the spring runoff peak rather than the temperature peak."
- shared_context: summer algal blooms occur in Lake X
- contrast_variable: timing of bloom onset relative to runoff peak and temperature peak
- contrast_direction_or_state: onset follows the runoff peak
- system_or_population: Lake X
- measurement_or_observable: dated bloom-onset records against runoff and temperature
  time series

Return a single JSON object, and nothing else:

{
  "has_contrast": <true|false>,
  "shared_context": "<...>",
  "contrast_variable": "<...>",
  "contrast_direction_or_state": "<...>",
  "system_or_population": "<...>",
  "measurement_or_observable": "<...>"
}
