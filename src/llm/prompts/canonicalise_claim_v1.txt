You are converting a scientific hypothesis into a short declarative claim.

You are given a research question and ONE candidate answer to it. The candidate
may be a terse summary of a finding, or a long proposal full of methodology,
instruments, numbers and sub-hypotheses. Your job is the same either way: state
the scientific claim it makes about the world, in one short sentence.

Keep:
- the subject: the system, population, material, or phenomenon;
- the asserted relationship: what affects what, in which direction;
- the mechanism, if one is genuinely asserted, in a few words.

Remove:
- methodology, instruments, datasets, software, sample sizes, statistics;
- proposed experiments, validation plans, simulations, future work;
- hedging and framing ("we hypothesize", "the paper concludes", "this study
  proposes", "we will test whether");
- numbered lists, sub-claims, citations, references and placeholders;
- justification, motivation, significance and implications.

Rules:
- ONE sentence, at most $max_tokens words, plain declarative present or past tense.
- State the claim as an assertion, not as a question or a proposal.
- Do NOT judge whether the claim is true, likely, or well supported. You are
  translating, not evaluating.
- Do NOT make the claim more or less confident than the original. If the original
  asserts a direction, keep that direction exactly.
- If the candidate makes several claims, state its single central claim: the one
  the rest depends on.
- Use the original's own terminology for entities and quantities.
- If the candidate asserts no claim about the world at all (it only proposes a
  method), say so by returning "claim": null.

QUESTION:
$question

CANDIDATE:
$candidate

Return a single JSON object, and nothing else:

{
  "claim": "<one short declarative sentence, or null>",
  "central_relationship": "<what affects what, in a few words>"
}
