You are assessing what a retrieved body of scientific literature says about one
candidate hypothesis.

You are given a scientific question, one candidate hypothesis, and the complete
set of literature available to you for this assessment. Judge the retrieved
studies jointly: you are not counting papers, you are assessing what this body of
evidence implies about the hypothesis.

Consider:
- relevance of each study to the claim actually made by the hypothesis;
- how direct the reported measurements are;
- methodological strength, where it can be inferred;
- consistency and disagreement across studies;
- whether several records are independent studies or repeated reports of the
  same underlying result (same cohort, same dataset, same group, preprint and
  journal version of one work).

Important:
- If the retrieved literature does not bear on the hypothesis, the correct label
  is "no_evidence". Absence of evidence is NOT evidence against the hypothesis,
  and must not be reported as a contradiction.
- "mixed" means informative studies were found that point in conflicting
  directions. It is not the same as "no_evidence".
- Judge only what the retrieved records support. Do not substitute your own
  recollection of the literature for the records shown, and do not cite studies
  that are not listed below.

QUESTION:
$question

CANDIDATE HYPOTHESIS:
$hypothesis

RETRIEVED LITERATURE:
$literature

Return a single JSON object, and nothing else:

{
  "evidence_label": "<one of: $labels>",
  "score": <integer 0-100>,
  "rationale": "<2-4 sentences explaining the judgment>",
  "key_papers": ["<id of each record that drove the judgment>"],
  "independence_note": "<how many effectively independent studies the evidence represents, or null>"
}

Where:
- "evidence_label" describes the direction and strength of the retrieved evidence
  with respect to the hypothesis.
- "score" is your probability, on a 0-100 scale, that this hypothesis is correct
  given the question and the retrieved evidence. If the label is "no_evidence",
  the score should reflect your prior judgment of the hypothesis and should not
  be lowered merely because nothing was retrieved.
- "key_papers" uses the `id:` values shown with each record, and must be empty
  when the label is "no_evidence".
