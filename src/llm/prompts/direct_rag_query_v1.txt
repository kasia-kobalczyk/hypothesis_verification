You are generating scientific literature search queries.

Given a scientific question and one candidate hypothesis, write $n_queries search
queries that would retrieve studies bearing on whether the hypothesis holds.

The queries must be OUTCOME-NEUTRAL. Describe the entities, systems,
interventions, comparisons and measured quantities involved, without encoding
the outcome that the hypothesis predicts, and without asking for confirmation or
refutation.

Acceptable:
  M mutation phospho-RPA cells
  M mutant RPA phosphorylation

Not acceptable:
  evidence that M increases phospho-RPA
  proof that M causes replication stress
  studies confirming the hypothesis

Further requirements:
- Write queries as keyword phrases suitable for a scientific search engine, not
  as questions or sentences.
- Use standard scientific terminology for the entities and measurements.
- Each query should target a different aspect, wording, or measurement of the
  claim; do not paraphrase the same query twice.
- Do not include years, date ranges, or words like "recent" or "latest".
- Do not name specific papers, authors, or journals.

QUESTION:
$question

CANDIDATE HYPOTHESIS:
$hypothesis

Return a single JSON object, and nothing else:

{
  "queries": ["<query 1>", "<query 2>"]
}
