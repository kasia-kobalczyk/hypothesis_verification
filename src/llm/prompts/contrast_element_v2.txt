You are identifying the part of a scientific proposition that could tell candidate
explanations apart, and checking whether the candidates really disagree about it. You are
NOT judging whether the proposition is true.

CANDIDATE EXPLANATIONS:
$candidates

PROPOSITION:
$proposition

Step 1. Decompose the proposition into:

- shared_context: background that every candidate accepts or that holds whichever candidate
  is correct (the system, the general setting, things no candidate disputes);
- contrast_variable: the specific variable, ordering, state or relation whose value would
  differ depending on which candidate is correct. This is the piece that could make the
  proposition discriminative;
- contrast_direction_or_state: the value of that variable which the proposition asserts;
- system_or_population: where it is asserted to hold;
- measurement_or_observable: what one would measure or observe to establish the
  contrast_variable's value.

Keep each field short and concrete. Do not add claims the proposition does not make.

Step 2. For EACH candidate, judge what its OWN content requires about the contrast_variable,
using only what the candidate explanation states:

- "requires_asserted": the candidate's own content requires the asserted value, or makes it
  clearly expected. You can point to the words of the candidate that do so;
- "requires_other": the candidate's own content rules out the asserted value, or makes it
  clearly unexpected. You can point to the words of the candidate that do so;
- "not_required": the candidate is compatible with the asserted value AND with a different
  value. Use this when the candidate does not fix the magnitude, timing, prevalence,
  generality or detectability that the asserted value needs, or when the asserted value
  only follows from the candidate together with assumptions the candidate does not state.

Being silent is "not_required". A value that another candidate explains more naturally is
not thereby "requires_other" for this candidate: something in this candidate must rule it out.

Illustration (a dispute that is not part of this task). Candidates about why Lake X began
having summer algal blooms: A = nutrient runoff from new farmland; B = rising water temperature.

Proposition: "Across years, blooms in Lake X occurred in the years of highest phosphorus
loading rather than in the warmest years."
- shared_context: summer algal blooms occur in Lake X in some years
- contrast_variable: which inter-annual driver bloom years track
- contrast_direction_or_state: bloom years track phosphorus loading, not temperature
- system_or_population: Lake X, across years
- measurement_or_observable: bloom occurrence per year against annual phosphorus loading and
  annual water temperature
- A: requires_asserted; B: requires_other

Proposition: "Dissolved phosphorus in Lake X is higher during blooms than between them."
- A: requires_asserted; B: not_required (warming does not fix phosphorus levels during
  blooms either way)

Return a single JSON object, and nothing else:

{
  "shared_context": "<...>",
  "contrast_variable": "<...>",
  "contrast_direction_or_state": "<...>",
  "system_or_population": "<...>",
  "measurement_or_observable": "<...>",
  "candidate_positions": [
    {"id": "<candidate label>", "position": "<requires_asserted|requires_other|not_required>",
     "reason": "<one sentence>"}
  ]
}
