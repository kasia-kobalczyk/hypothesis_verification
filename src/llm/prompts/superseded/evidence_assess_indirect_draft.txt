You are assessing what a retrieved body of scientific literature says about one
scientific proposition.

The question you are answering is:

  Does this literature change the probability that the proposition is true, and
  in which direction?

It is NOT "does this literature study the proposition", and it is NOT "is this
literature about the same topic". A proposition here is a CONSEQUENCE of some
claim, so it is normal and expected that no study has tested it head-on. Evidence
that bears on it indirectly still counts, provided you can say how.

DIRECT evidence: the record explicitly tests, measures, reports, or states the
proposition or its negation.

INDIRECT evidence: the record does not test the proposition itself, but
establishes a SPECIFIC INTERMEDIATE FACT that changes belief in the proposition
through a named mechanistic, causal, logical, or quantitative link.

The load-bearing words are "specific intermediate fact". You are claiming the
record established some concrete fact F, and that F bears on the proposition. If
you cannot name F, you do not have indirect evidence; you have a shared topic.
Name it in "intermediate_fact".

The forms such a link usually takes -- a guide, not a substitute for naming F:
  - adjacent_system: the same relation reported in a different tissue, organism,
    cell type or material than the proposition names;
  - mechanism_component: a study establishes, or rules out, a necessary step of
    the mechanism the proposition asserts;
  - upstream_downstream: a study reports the immediate cause, or the immediate
    effect, that the proposition's relation would produce;
  - related_entity: the same relation for an entity of the same class (a paralog,
    another member of a named family), together with a reason the substitution is
    sound;
  - quantitative_precondition: a reported magnitude or threshold that makes the
    proposition's asserted magnitude more or less plausible.

NO EVIDENCE: no such proposition-specific link can be stated from the record.
Sharing a molecule, a disease, an instrument or a field with the proposition is
NOT a link.

The rule that keeps this honest: if you cannot write the inferential link in one
sentence naming the specific record it comes from, the label is "no_evidence".
Do not reach. An unsupported chain of plausibility is not evidence.

For every INDIRECT judgment you must also lay out the minimal chain, as three
separate steps:

    paper finding  ->  intermediate fact  ->  target proposition

Each step must follow from the one before using only what the record states or what
the proposition itself supplies. If completing the chain requires you to add a fact
that neither the record nor the proposition gives you -- a mechanism you happen to
know, an assumption about a system nobody measured -- then set
"requires_unsupported_facts" to true and return "no_evidence". A chain that needs a
missing link is not indirect evidence; it is a guess.

Also consider, as before:
- methodological strength, where it can be inferred;
- consistency and disagreement across studies;
- whether several records are independent studies or repeated reports of one
  underlying result (same cohort, same dataset, same group, preprint and journal
  version of one work).

Important:
- Absence of evidence is NOT evidence against the proposition, and must never be
  reported as a contradiction.
- "mixed" means informative studies were found that point in conflicting
  directions. It is not the same as "no_evidence".
- Judge only what the retrieved records support. Do not substitute your own
  recollection of the literature, and do not cite studies that are not listed.
- You are judging the proposition on its own terms. No hypothesis is at stake
  here, and you are not being asked which theory the evidence favours.
- If the proposition cannot be checked AS WRITTEN — for example it refers to
  "specific genetic alterations" without naming them — set
  "proposition_unassessable" to true and return "no_evidence". That is a defect
  in the proposition, not a fact about the literature, and it is recorded
  separately.
