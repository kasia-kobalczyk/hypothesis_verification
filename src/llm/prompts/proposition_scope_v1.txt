You are classifying the SCOPE of one scientific proposition relative to a set of
candidate explanations of a phenomenon. You are NOT judging whether the proposition is
true, and NOT judging which candidate is right.

PHENOMENON UNDER DISPUTE:
$question

CANDIDATE EXPLANATIONS:
$candidates

PROPOSITION:
$proposition

Classify the proposition into exactly one scope:

- "hypothesis_specific": it directly instantiates a distinctive commitment of one or more
  candidates, in the specific system or phenomenon under dispute.
- "mechanism_specific": it tests a mechanism that is central to a candidate explanation,
  and it stays meaningfully diagnostic for the system under dispute, even though it is
  phrased in mechanistic rather than case-specific terms.
- "broader_class_fact": it states a general property of a wider class, family or kind of
  system, which could hold whichever candidate is correct for the system under dispute.
- "possibility_claim": it only states that something CAN, MAY or COULD occur, or is
  possible, somewhere or in some related system, without asserting that it governs the
  phenomenon under dispute.
- "invalid_or_underspecified": it is too vague, not operationally meaningful, or not
  about anything the candidates' dispute depends on.

Rules:
1. Judge what the proposition asserts, not where it came from. A proposition derived from
   one candidate is not "hypothesis_specific" for that reason alone.
2. "Can", "may", "is possible", "is capable of" usually signal "possibility_claim", unless
   the proposition also asserts that this is what happens in the system under dispute.
3. A statement about a whole family or class is "broader_class_fact" unless the candidate
   explanations themselves are claims about that whole class.
4. "mechanism_specific" requires that the mechanism is central to a candidate AND that
   the proposition would actually tell the candidates apart for the system under dispute.
   A mechanism that operates generally, and would be expected under every candidate, is a
   "broader_class_fact".

Illustration (a dispute that is not part of this task). Phenomenon: why a particular lake,
Lake X, began having summer algal blooms. Candidates: nutrient runoff from new farmland vs
rising water temperature.
- "Phosphorus loading can trigger algal blooms in freshwater lakes." -> possibility_claim
- "Algal bloom frequency in temperate lakes generally increases with water temperature."
  -> broader_class_fact
- "In Lake X, bloom onset followed the spring runoff peak rather than the temperature
  peak." -> hypothesis_specific
- "Dissolved phosphorus in Lake X rose before each bloom began." -> mechanism_specific

Return a single JSON object, and nothing else:

{
  "scope": "<hypothesis_specific|mechanism_specific|broader_class_fact|possibility_claim|invalid_or_underspecified>",
  "system_under_dispute": "<the system or phenomenon the candidates are about, briefly>",
  "proposition_is_about": "<the system, class or setting the proposition is about, briefly>",
  "asserts_actual_occurrence_in_system_under_dispute": <true|false>,
  "rationale": "<one or two sentences>"
}
