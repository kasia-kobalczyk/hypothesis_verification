You are screening a pair of scientific hypotheses for a benchmark.

Both hypotheses were written in response to the same scientific question. The
first ("GOLD") states what a published study actually concluded. The second
("ALTERNATIVE") is a competing candidate. Your job is to decide whether this is a
meaningful pair to compare — not which one is correct.

Apply exactly two criteria:

1. SAME SCIENTIFIC TARGET.
   The alternative addresses the same scientific target as the gold: the same
   system, population, phenomenon or quantity, in a way that makes the two
   claims about the same thing. An alternative that changes the subject, answers
   a different question, or only proposes methodology without committing to an
   outcome on the gold's target does NOT satisfy this.

2. SUBSTANTIVE DISAGREEMENT.
   The alternative disagrees with a substantive part of the gold: it asserts a
   different direction, mechanism, magnitude, or conclusion, such that the two
   cannot both be straightforwardly correct. An alternative that merely adds
   detail, restates the gold in other words, or is fully compatible with it does
   NOT satisfy this.

Notes:
- Do not judge which hypothesis is scientifically correct.
- Do not reward or penalise length, formatting, technical detail, confidence of
  tone, or the presence of numbers and citations. Elaborate alternatives and
  terse ones are judged by the same two criteria.
- If the alternative is internally incoherent or largely unintelligible, it
  cannot address the same target: mark criterion 1 false.
- Judge only the two texts shown.

QUESTION:
$question

GOLD:
$gold

ALTERNATIVE:
$alternative

Return a single JSON object, and nothing else:

{
  "same_target": true or false,
  "disagrees_substantively": true or false,
  "decision": "PASS" or "FAIL",
  "rationale": "<one or two sentences naming the specific point of agreement or disagreement>"
}

"decision" is "PASS" only when both criteria are true.
