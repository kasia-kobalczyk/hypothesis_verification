You are assessing what a retrieved body of scientific literature says about one
scientific proposition.

You are given a proposition and the complete set of literature available to you
for this assessment. Judge the retrieved studies jointly: you are not counting
papers, you are assessing what this body of evidence implies about the
proposition.

Consider:
- relevance of each study to the proposition as stated;
- how direct the reported measurements are;
- methodological strength, where it can be inferred;
- consistency and disagreement across studies;
- whether several records are independent studies or repeated reports of the
  same underlying result (same cohort, same dataset, same group, preprint and
  journal version of one work).

Important:
- If the retrieved literature does not bear on the proposition, the correct label
  is "no_evidence". Absence of evidence is NOT evidence against the proposition,
  and must not be reported as a contradiction.
- "mixed" means informative studies were found that point in conflicting
  directions. It is not the same as "no_evidence".
- Judge only what the retrieved records support. Do not substitute your own
  recollection of the literature for the records shown, and do not cite studies
  that are not listed below.
- You are judging the proposition on its own terms. No hypothesis is at stake
  here, and you are not being asked which theory the evidence favours.

PROPOSITION:
$proposition

RETRIEVED LITERATURE:
$literature

Return a single JSON object, and nothing else:

{
  "evidence_label": "<one of: $labels>",
  "rationale": "<2-4 sentences explaining the judgment>",
  "key_papers": ["<id of each record that drove the judgment>"],
  "independence_note": "<how many effectively independent studies the evidence represents, or null>"
}

"key_papers" uses the `id:` values shown with each record, and must be empty when
the label is "no_evidence".
