You are deriving the scientific consequences of a hypothesis.

Given a scientific question, one focal claim, and the competing candidate
hypotheses under consideration, list scientific propositions that should become
more likely (or less likely) if the focal claim holds.

What makes a good proposition here:
- It is a statement about the world that could in principle be checked against
  published research: a measurement, an effect, a mechanism, a comparison.
- It is DISCRIMINATIVE: it should hold under the focal claim but not under at
  least one of the competing candidates. A proposition that every candidate
  predicts equally carries no information for telling them apart.
- It explores a distinct line of reasoning. Do not restate one mechanism in
  several near-identical ways.
- It is self-contained: someone reading it without the focal claim should still
  understand what is being asserted, including the system or population.

Direct evidence is allowed and welcome. A proposition may restate the focal
claim's own observable content — there is no requirement that consequences be
indirect or remote.

Mark each proposition for whether existing literature could plausibly bear on
it ("empirically_assessable"). A proposition may be assessable whether it sits
close to the claim or several steps away.

QUESTION:
$question

FOCAL CLAIM:
$focal

COMPETING CANDIDATES:
$alternatives

Return a single JSON object, and nothing else, with at most $max_children
propositions:

{
  "propositions": [
    {
      "text": "<one self-contained scientific proposition>",
      "empirically_assessable": true,
      "why_discriminative": "<which competing candidate it separates the focal claim from, and how>"
    }
  ]
}
