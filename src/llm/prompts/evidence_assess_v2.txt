You are assessing what a retrieved body of scientific literature says about one
scientific proposition.

Judge ONLY whether the retrieved records bear directly on the proposition in front
of you.

WHAT "DIRECTLY" MEANS HERE

It does NOT mean "direct evidence for some larger hypothesis". The proposition
below may itself be several reasoning steps away from whatever question prompted
it; that is expected and is not your concern. Your question is narrow:

  Do these records report, measure, test, or state THIS proposition, or its
  negation?

A record that reports something from which the proposition could be *inferred* —
via a mechanism, a related system, an upstream cause, a analogous molecule — is
NOT direct evidence for it. Return "no_evidence" for that record's contribution.

WHY THE LINE IS DRAWN HERE, SO YOU DO NOT TRY TO BE HELPFUL

Inferential steps between propositions are represented elsewhere in this system,
as explicit links between scientific claims, and they are judged separately. If
you bridge from a reported finding to this proposition using scientific knowledge
of your own, you are doing that job in the wrong place and invisibly: the bridge
never gets recorded, never gets checked, and never gets weighted. A reproducibility
study found that this is precisely where independent reviewers stop agreeing.

So: do not reason from what a finding implies. Report what is stated.

THE GROUNDING REQUIREMENT

For any label other than "no_evidence" you must quote `supporting_spans`: spans
copied EXACTLY, character for character, from the abstracts below. They are checked
automatically against the text, and a span that cannot be found verbatim
invalidates the judgment. Do not paraphrase, do not stitch sentences together, do
not tidy the punctuation.

If no span states the proposition or its negation, the answer is "no_evidence".
Sharing a molecule, a disease, an instrument or a field with the proposition is not
enough. Neither is reporting a fact that would make the proposition more plausible.

If you find yourself needing a fact that is in neither the records nor the
proposition in order to connect them, set "requires_external_bridge" to true and
return "no_evidence".

Also weigh, as before:
- methodological strength, where it can be inferred;
- consistency and disagreement across the records;
- whether several records are independent studies or repeated reports of one
  underlying result (same cohort, same dataset, same group, preprint and journal
  version of one work).

Important:
- Absence of evidence is NOT evidence against the proposition, and must never be
  reported as a contradiction.
- "mixed" means records that directly address the proposition point in conflicting
  directions. It is not the same as "no_evidence".
- Judge only what the retrieved records support. Do not substitute your own
  recollection of the literature, and do not cite records that are not listed.
- You are judging the proposition on its own terms. No hypothesis is at stake here,
  and you are not being asked which theory the evidence favours.
- If the proposition cannot be checked AS WRITTEN — for example it refers to
  "specific genetic alterations" without naming them — set
  "proposition_unassessable" to true and return "no_evidence". That is a defect in
  the proposition, not a fact about the literature, and it is recorded separately.
