You are classifying what one retrieved scientific record says about one proposition.

Work in this order: decide the category first from the definitions, then justify it.

THE THREE CATEGORIES

DIRECT — the record explicitly tests, measures, reports, or states the proposition
or its negation.

INDIRECT — the record does not test the proposition itself, but establishes a
SPECIFIC INTERMEDIATE FACT that changes belief in the proposition through a named
mechanistic, causal, logical, or quantitative link.

NO_EVIDENCE — no such proposition-specific link can be stated from the record.

WHAT YOU ARE AND ARE NOT BEING ASKED

You are NOT being asked whether the proposition is true in the world, and you are
NOT being asked to draw on what you know about the field. You are being asked
whether THIS SUPPLIED RECORD supports the intermediate fact you name. Everything
must come from the text below.

THE GROUNDING REQUIREMENT

For any DIRECT or INDIRECT judgment you must quote a `supporting_span`: a
contiguous span of text copied EXACTLY, character for character, from the abstract
below. It will be checked automatically against the abstract, and a span that is
not found verbatim invalidates the judgment. Do not paraphrase, do not join two
sentences, do not fix typography.

If no span in the abstract states the fact you want to rely on, the answer is
NO_EVIDENCE. Sharing a molecule, a disease, an instrument or a field with the
proposition is not a link.

If completing the reasoning requires a fact that neither the record nor the
proposition supplies — a mechanism you happen to know, an assumption about a
system nobody measured — set "requires_unsupported_facts" to true and answer
NO_EVIDENCE.

Return a single JSON object, and nothing else:

{
  "category": "<DIRECT, INDIRECT, or NO_EVIDENCE>",
  "direction": "<supports, contradicts, or none>",

Set "merely_related" to true when the record shares subject matter with the
proposition but you cannot state a link. That is different from a record that is
simply off-topic.
