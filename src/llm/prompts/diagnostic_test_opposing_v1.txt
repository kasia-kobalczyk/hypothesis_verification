Find measurements on which two scientific claims make CONFLICTING predictions.

This is a narrow task. You are looking only for measurements where BOTH claims
determine an outcome, and those outcomes DISAGREE. For example:

    claim 1 implies the quantity goes UP,   claim 2 implies it goes DOWN
    claim 1 implies the quantity goes UP,   claim 2 implies it stays at baseline

That is what makes a measurement able to tell the claims apart.

THE CASE THAT DOES NOT COUNT

A measurement where one claim predicts something and the other is SILENT is not
what you are looking for. If claim 2 is simply about a different mechanism,
different system or different quantity, then it makes no prediction here, and
observing the outcome would not tell you which claim is right.

Do NOT convert silence into a prediction. Do not write "claim 2 implies no change"
because claim 2 does not mention the quantity. A claim implies no change only when
it positively rules the effect out -- for instance a claim that a factor plays no
role implies that removing that factor changes nothing.

RETURNING ZERO IS A CORRECT ANSWER

Two claims can both be substantive and still never disagree about any single
measurable quantity -- they may describe parallel mechanisms, or address different
aspects of the same question. If that is the case here, return an empty list. Do
not manufacture a conflict to fill the quota. An empty answer is more useful than
an invented one.

WHAT TO WRITE FOR EACH MEASUREMENT

  system                    what is studied
  context                   conditions: dose, duration, temperature, cohort
  intervention_or_exposure  what is varied, applied, removed or compared
  measured_outcome          the single quantity measured, stated precisely
  baseline_or_comparator    what "increase" and "decrease" are relative to
  why_claim_1_predicts      why the FIRST claim determines this outcome
  why_claim_2_predicts      why the SECOND claim determines this outcome
  expected_conflict         one sentence on how the two predictions differ

Both "why" fields must be real reasoning from the claim's own content. If you
cannot write one of them honestly, that measurement does not belong in your answer.

Do NOT state the predicted directions themselves. The directions are assigned
later, independently, by assessors who see one claim at a time. Your job is to
propose the measurement and say why each claim bears on it.

You have no literature access. Do not consider what studies have found, or which
claim is actually correct.
