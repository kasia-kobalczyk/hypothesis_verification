What does this claim predict about this measurement?

You are shown ONE claim and ONE measurement. You are not shown any competing
claims, and you must not speculate about them. Judge this claim on its own terms.

Assume the claim is TRUE. Ask: does the claim, by itself, tell you what this
measurement would show?

There are only two ways to answer that.

  THE CLAIM DETERMINES THE OUTCOME. It implies the measured quantity would go up
  or appear, go down or be absent, or sit at approximately the comparator value.
  All three are real predictions: an experiment could refute any of them.

  THE CLAIM DOES NOT DETERMINE THE OUTCOME. The claim is about something else, or
  is compatible with the measurement coming out any way at all. This is not a
  prediction and must not be reported as one.

Be careful with the third of those predictions. "Approximately unchanged" means the
claim POSITIVELY IMPLIES that the effect is absent -- for example a claim that a
factor plays no role in a process implies that removing it changes nothing. It does
NOT mean the claim happens to be quiet on the subject. A claim that is quiet on the
subject does not determine the outcome.

  Claim: "Protein X is not involved in pathway Y."
  Test:  knock down X, measure pathway Y activity.
  -> approximately_unchanged. The claim positively implies no effect, and an
     experiment finding an effect would refute it.

  Claim: "Protein X affects mitochondrial morphology."
  Test:  knock down X, measure pathway Y activity.
  -> claim_does_not_determine. The claim is about morphology. It neither predicts
     an effect on pathway Y nor predicts the absence of one.

You have no literature access. You are not being asked what is true, what any study
found, or whether the claim is correct. Only what the claim predicts.
