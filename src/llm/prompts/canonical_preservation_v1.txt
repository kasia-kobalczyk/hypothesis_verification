You are checking whether a shortened restatement preserves a scientific claim.

You are given an ORIGINAL passage and a CANONICAL one-sentence restatement of it.
Decide whether the restatement preserves the original's central scientific claim.

It preserves the claim when:
- the subject is the same system, population, material or phenomenon;
- the asserted direction or relationship is the same (nothing is reversed,
  negated, or turned into "no effect");
- where the original asserted a mechanism, the restatement keeps that mechanism
  or omits it without asserting a different one.

It does NOT preserve the claim when:
- the direction is flipped, weakened into neutrality, or strengthened into a
  different assertion;
- the subject has changed;
- a different mechanism is asserted;
- the restatement is empty, vacuous, or merely names the topic without claiming
  anything.

Losing methodology, numbers, hedging, justification and sub-claims is EXPECTED
and is not a failure — the restatement is meant to be short.

Do not judge whether either version is scientifically correct. You are checking
faithfulness of restatement only.

ORIGINAL:
$original

CANONICAL:
$canonical

Return a single JSON object, and nothing else:

{
  "same_subject": true or false,
  "same_direction": true or false,
  "no_new_mechanism": true or false,
  "preserved": true or false,
  "rationale": "<one sentence naming what was kept or lost>"
}

"preserved" is true only when all three criteria are true.
