## 4. Milestone 2 — the consequence verifier runs end to end

**Done.** Six instances, **144 propositions**, 0 retrieval failures, 0 unassessed
nodes, `status=ok` throughout. Both paths exercised: five v2 pairs (k=2) and one
v1 instance (k=11, 44 nodes, 495 edges, of which 484 = 44 × 11 root edges — so
cross-evaluation scales). Full write-up in `docs/MILESTONE_2.md`.

### The finding that matters

**Assessment yield collapses as the candidate count rises.**

| slice | nodes | informative evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| k=2 | 100 | **40%** |
| k=11 | 44 | **4%** |
| depth 1 | 63 | 30% |
| depth 2 | 81 | 28% |
| ≥8 of 10 abstracts available | 53 | 38% |
| ≤4 of 10 abstracts available | 22 | 27% |

Depth is ruled out (30% vs 28%). Retrieval substrate is a minor factor (11 points,
and the k=11 instance had normal abstract coverage). What is left is proposition
*specificity*: asked to distinguish one claim from ten others, the model writes
five-clause conjunctions that no abstract can settle, and the assessor correctly
returns `no_evidence`. With 42 of 44 nodes contributing exactly zero, RBV-19's
posterior is nearly flat (top four: 0.106, 0.100, 0.100, 0.094) — even though the
graph was discriminative *a priori* (spread of `P(X_v|H)` median 0.50, max 0.85,
no degenerate node).

Discriminativeness and assessability pull against each other. The principled fix
is to decompose — atomic propositions, discrimination supplied by
cross-evaluation, which is already a separate stage. **That changes the generation
prompt, so I did not do it**; it is written up as `DECISIONS #17` with the three
options and the reason for rejecting the easy one (raising `max_nodes` just buys
more zeros).

### Two further findings, both recorded not patched

- **Merging is inert and lowering the threshold will not fix it.** Zero merges,
  zero shared nodes across 144 propositions. Cross-hypothesis proposition
  similarity maxes at **0.460**, so even a threshold of 0.60 — far below the 0.82
  placeholder — would merge nothing. The generation prompt's discriminativeness
  instruction suppresses the shared consequences the method expects. Needs a
  research decision, not a config change.
- **The refuting half of the evidence scale is barely exercised**: 5 refutations
  in 144 assessments (2 `contradiction`, 3 `weak_contradiction`) against 36
  supports. A method meant to draw power from ruling candidates *out* is currently
  getting nearly all its signal from ruling them in. Six instances cannot separate
  "property of the domain" from "property of `evidence_assess_v1`".

### Fixed along the way

Graph runs emitted `n_informative` / `n_no_evidence` while the aggregator summed
`n_informative_assessments` / `n_no_evidence_assessments`, so aggregate
diagnostics came back `None` even though the per-instance artifacts had the
numbers. Renamed, added the missing keys to the aggregated set, and added a test
that fails if any emitted counter is one the aggregator ignores (`DECISIONS #18`).

## 5. Style-controlled benchmark by symmetric canonicalisation

**Done, protocol frozen first.** `docs/CANONICAL_PROTOCOL.md` was written and
frozen *before* the build ran; the transformation is applied blindly and
identically to every candidate, the role (gold/negative) is never passed to any
judge, and no literature search or verifier output touches selection.

`benchmark/canonical/`: **105 pairs / 21 rows**. Semantic preservation
**219/231 = 94.8%**.

Against the pre-declared pass criterion:

| diagnostic | v1 | v2 | canonical | verdict at within-0.1-of-chance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| shortest-text-first | 0.988 | 0.760 | **0.605** | **fail** |
| longest-text-first | 0.012 | 0.240 | **0.395** | **fail** |
| question-hidden judge | 0.785 | 0.890 | **0.686** | **fail** |
| gold shorter than negative | 98.4% | 74.0% | 57.1% | — |

**The slice fails the style diagnostic and is not yet usable as an evaluation
benchmark.** This corrects an earlier version of this report, which printed "within
0.1 of chance" as the criterion for both rows and then marked 0.686 a pass — see
`DECISIONS #16` for what the frozen protocol actually said and why the reading went
wrong. Short version: the protocol declared "near 0.5" and "substantially below
0.870" without numbers, and the 0.75 bar that turned 0.686 into a pass was chosen
after the result was known.

What canonicalisation does achieve is still substantial and measured: the length
cue drops 0.988 → 0.760 → 0.605 and the style cue 0.890 → 0.686, without changing
which claim is correct (preservation 94.8%). It just does not reach chance. The
residual is probably irreducible by canonicalisation alone — a correct hypothesis
and a fabricated one differ in how much there is to say. Minimal contrast pairs are
the route to closing it, and per your sequencing that waits until the verifier is
known to be viable.
