## Corrections to previously reported findings

### 1. Pair judgments were not counterbalanced, and one headline number was confounded

You asked whether pairs are scored both ways. They were not — each pair was presented
once, with a seeded coin flip deciding whether the gold was option A or B. That makes
the gold positionally unidentifiable, which is what it was built for, but it does not
remove **position preference in the judge**. Measured on the runs already on disk:

| slice | chose A | acc when gold@A | when gold@B | reported |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| v1 | 67% | 0.951 | 0.617 | 0.785 |
| v2 official | 61% | 0.980 | 0.760 | 0.870 |
| **canonical** | **85%** | 0.983 | **0.319** | 0.686 |

On canonical the judge answered "A" for 89 of 105 pairs, and
`0.552 × 0.983 + 0.448 × 0.319 = 0.686` reproduces the reported accuracy exactly. An
always-answer-A baseline scores 0.552 there.

Every pair is now judged in **both** orders and averaged, so a pure position-guesser
scores exactly 0.500. Re-run results:

| slice | single-order | counterbalanced | order consistency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| v1 | 0.785 | 0.777 | 0.736 |
| v2 official | 0.890 | **0.910** | 0.860 |
| canonical | 0.686 | **0.643** | **0.381** |

The decomposition is more informative than either number:

| slice | share of pairs with an order-stable answer | accuracy on those |
| --- | --- | --- |
| v2 official | 86% | 0.977 |
| canonical | **38%** | 0.875 |

**Canonicalisation cut the number of pairs carrying a usable text-only cue from 86% to
38%, rather than weakening the cue where it survives.** That is a better result than
0.686 suggested, and a more tractable target — the residual sits in a minority of
pairs. v2's leakage, conversely, is worse than reported: 0.910.

`--single-order` reproduces the old behaviour for provenance. The length heuristics are
deterministic and unaffected. (DECISIONS #21)

### 2. The canonical verdict was stated against a criterion the protocol never set

You caught this. The frozen protocol said "both length heuristics near 0.5, and the
question-hidden judge substantially below the 0.870 it scores on v2" — **no number on
either bar**. `write_canonical_report.py` operationalised "substantially below" as
`< 0.75`, and I chose that value *after* seeing 0.686. `STATUS_2026-09-11.md` then
printed "within 0.1 of chance" for both rows while marking 0.686 a pass, which is
self-contradictory on its face.

Both bars are now explicitly at within 0.1 of chance, applied to the verdict, with the
protocol's original text left unedited — rewriting a frozen pre-registration to match a
later decision is the error the entry exists to document. **Canonical fails all three
diagnostics** (0.605 / 0.395 / 0.643) and its manifest now says it is not usable as an
evaluation benchmark. Also worth knowing: the judge is not deterministic — the same
prompt over the same 100 v2 pairs scored 0.870 and 0.890 forty-three minutes apart, so
treat ~0.02 as noise. (DECISIONS #16)

### 3. `text_similarity` was asymmetric and autojunk-distorted

`SequenceMatcher(None, a, b).ratio()` applies an autojunk heuristic above 200
characters and keys it off its second argument alone. Over 1,323 proposition pairs:
asymmetric for **95%** of them (worst case 0.031 one way, 0.451 the other, for two
texts sharing a phrase verbatim), and autojunk shifted similarity by a mean of 0.234.

Fixed (`autojunk=False`, pair sorted before comparison). **This invalidated a stated
finding:** MILESTONE_2 had said a threshold of 0.60 would still merge nothing. With the
corrected function, 0.60 merges 3 cross-hypothesis pairs and 0.50 merges 44. The frozen
0.82 still merges nothing, so no earlier run changes — zero merges was the right answer
for the wrong reason. (DECISIONS #19)

## The method revision: `consequence_generate_v2`

Built exactly as specified — atomic, empirically assessable consequences, no
requirement that any proposition be discriminative, discrimination measured afterwards
by cross-evaluation. v1 kept and selectable via `graph.generate_prompt`.

Non-contrastiveness is **structural**: the v2 template does not declare
`$alternatives`, and `_generate_children` passes only the placeholders a template
declares, so the competing candidates cannot reach it whatever the prose says. A test
pins that.

Budget is now per-candidate: `max_root_consequences_per_hypothesis: 3`, with
`max_nodes` demoted to a safety cap that `build_graph` **refuses before spending a
single call** if it is too small to expand every candidate. Depth-2 expansion is
round-robin across candidates so truncation no longer gives every slot to whichever
candidate came first.

### k=2 A/B — same five instances, same node budget, only the prompt changed

| | v1 | v2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **informative evidence** | 40% | **49%** |
| excluding the over-merged instance | 42.5% | **55.1%** |
| median proposition tokens | 52 | **25** |
| clause markers per proposition | 1.44 | **0.08** |
| per-node discriminativeness (median) | 0.235 | 0.123 |
| nodes with zero discriminativeness | 0 / 100 | 2 / 91 |
| gold ranked first | 3 / 5 | 4 / 5 |

**Atomicity worked and is not subtle.** Length halved, conjunctions essentially
eliminated, and yield rose with them — the predicted causal chain. The cost is the
predicted one too: per-node discrimination halved, and two nodes became degenerate.
Whether *aggregate* discrimination survives is not answerable on five instances; the
two effects point opposite ways (gold first more often, but flatter posterior).

### k=11 A/B — atomicity did not help, and my explanation for why was confounded

`RBV-19`, same instance, budget matched: propositions went from 54 tokens to 18 and
clause markers from 2.05 to 0.07, and informative evidence went from 2 of 44 to **1 of
40**.

**The comparison behind DECISIONS #17 has to be withdrawn in the form I stated it.**
Both k=11 measurements are the same instance; the five k=2 measurements are five
different instances. k varied together with instance identity, so "40% at k=2 versus 4%
at k=11" was never separable from an instance effect — and it now looks like one.

What is actually limiting `RBV-19`: the propositions are fine, the queries are clean,
retrieval returns topically adjacent papers, and the assessor declines every one with
*"None of the retrieved literature **directly** investigates…"*. It is right about the
literature — the cutoff is 2023-05-07 and IL-10-driven sphingolipid metabolism in
macrophages was novel, which is why the source paper exists. But **requiring direct
address excludes exactly the indirect evidence a consequence graph is built to
accumulate.**

You said to leave the assessment prompt alone until atomicity was fixed and then
measure again. It is fixed; this is the re-measurement, and it points at assessor
strictness rather than proposition shape. Three options are written up in DECISIONS
#24; the one I would pick adds an explicit `indirect_support` level to the ordinal
scale — which needs calibration alongside it, so not on five instances.

### The clear win: shared consequences appeared

Cross-hypothesis proposition similarity at k=11 rose from 0.677 to **0.803**, and the
first shared node appeared. Different candidates now independently generate the same
consequence, and three of the four merges on that instance are genuine paraphrase
duplicates. That is the §35.9 behaviour you predicted would emerge once generation
stopped being asked to be distinctive.

### The blocker: merging is now actively harmful

I did not touch the threshold, as instructed. But merging went from never firing to
firing often, and **8 of 9 merges at k=2 collapsed scientifically distinct
propositions**:

| sim | kept | merged away |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0.944** | "**Autophagy** is activated in cancer cells undergoing disulfidptosis…" | "**Necroptosis** is activated…" |
| 0.893 | "altered NADPH/NADP+ ratios" | "**lower** NADPH/NADP+ in **heart** tissue" |
| 0.883 | "altered NADPH/NADP+ ratios" | "**higher** NADPH/NADP+ in **liver** tissue" |
| 0.852 | "**G6PD** activity is reduced…" | "**IDH** activity is altered…" |

Two of those collapse *opposite directional claims about different tissues* into one
node. The cause is the atomicity itself: atomic propositions share a syntactic template
and differ only in the entity, tissue or direction — a small fraction of the characters
and all of the science. **No threshold fixes this**: the worst case is 0.944, and a bar
above that merges nothing at all, including the one genuine duplicate at 0.882.

Recorded as a strict-xfail test that will flip to a failure when the matcher improves.
**My recommendation: `semantic_merge_mode: none` while generation is atomic** —
cross-evaluation already makes propositions that every candidate predicts inert, so
lexical merging is trading real distinctions for redundancy removal at one good merge in
nine. (DECISIONS #23)

### The nested k sweep answers the confound

Four rows from the balanced panel, run at k=2 and k=4 with nested candidates — same
rows, same negatives, only the set size differs.

| k | nodes | edges | median proposition words | informative | queries | merges/inst | cost/inst |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 22 | 58 | 18 | **44%** | 43 | 1.8 | $0.30 |
| 4 | 42 | 197 | 18 | **35%** | 84 | 4.5 | $0.62 |

**There is a real k effect on yield, and it is about nine points from k=2 to k=4 — not
the 36 points the confounded comparison implied.** Informative nodes rise absolutely
(38 → 59) but node count rises faster (86 → 167).

**Proposition length is identical at 18 words for both k.** Under v1 the model answered
a bigger candidate set with longer, more conjunctive propositions; under v2 it does not.
So the mechanism blamed in DECISIONS #17 is genuinely closed — it just was not what was
suppressing yield on `RBV-19`.

**Your metric design paid off immediately.** At k=4, top-1 is **0.250 — exactly chance —
while the pairwise win rate is 0.750**. The gold is rarely first and almost never last. A
pooled top-1 would have reported "no better than chance"; both k-independent metrics say
the opposite.

**And the merge defect scales with k**: 1.8 merges per instance at k=2, **4.5 at k=4** (7
against 18 over the same four rows). Since 8 of 9 merges were wrong, this makes it a
blocker for the scalability analysis, not a side issue.

Caveats: four rows, development slice, uncalibrated mappings, and every graph carries
corrupted nodes from merging. The scaling columns are trustworthy; the ranking columns
are not accuracy evidence.

## Evaluation protocol

Written up in `docs/EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md`, structured as you framed it: the
**formulation** covers arbitrary `k ≥ 2`, the **protocol** is a separate matter.

* **Primary: k=2 pairwise**, ties scored 0.5 explicitly. Already the case in both
  `pair_metrics` and `pairwise_accuracy_all_negatives`; now stated and tested.
* **Top-1, MRR and mean gold rank are reported only inside `by_k`**, each beside its own
  `chance_top1 = 1/k`. Averaging top-1 across mixed k is now impossible to do by
  accident.
* **Two metrics are pooled** because their chance level is k-independent: normalised
  gold rank `1 − (rank−1)/(k−1)` and the pairwise win rate inside a listwise task.
* k>2 is described as a different, more constrained ranking problem — not as "harder".

### Controlled listwise slices

`benchmark/k_slices/`, built by `scripts/build_k_slices.py` from negatives that
independently passed the frozen R2 criterion. Sampling frozen at build time (seed
recorded), and **nested**: the set at k takes the first k−1 negatives of a frozen
per-row order, so `H1` at k=3 is the same hypothesis as `H1` at k=2 and k is the only
thing that changes within a row.

| k | sets | rows short of k |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 62 | 0 |
| 3 | 27 | 35 |
| 4 | 18 | 44 |
| balanced panel (present at every k) | **18 rows** | — |

**k=5 is not built**: only 7 rows have ≥4 R2-passing negatives. That is a limit of how
much screening was done — the v2 screen stopped at its 100-pair target after 137 of 962
rows — not of the dataset. Reaching k=5 means screening more rows, not manufacturing
negatives.

## The assessor gate (added after your review)

The construct is now settled in `docs/ASSESSOR_RUBRIC.md` on your definitions —
`INDIRECT` anchored on a **specific intermediate fact**, not on a taxonomy of link
shapes (my first draft's weakness: a shape can be asserted without naming anything).
Two orthogonal axes, direction and directness, with the weighting deliberately not in
the taxonomy.

**The minimal-chain requirement is in**, as you proposed: every indirect judgment must
emit `paper finding -> intermediate fact -> target proposition` plus
`requires_unsupported_facts`, and a chain needing a fact nobody supplied returns
`no_evidence`. The harness counts truncated chains and flags admitted-unsupported ones
as rubric violations. That is what makes "indirect" auditable rather than rhetorical.

**`proposition_unassessable` moved to a separate upstream channel**, never an evidence
verdict — "retrieval found nothing" and "the proposition was underspecified" imply
different fixes, so they are counted apart.

**Adjudication is blind and dual.** `scripts/build_adjudication_packet.py` strips v1's
label and rationale and writes two independent worksheets over the 42 cases, with the
key in a separate file. `scripts/adjudication_agreement.py` reports Cohen's kappa and,
as the headline, **`boundary_agreement`** — agreement restricted to cases where at
least one judge said `indirect` or `no_evidence`, excluding easy `direct` cases that
inflate a raw number. That is your question ("can humans reliably distinguish indirect
but evidential from merely related?") made into a statistic.

**The gate is pre-registered and currently refuses to run.**
`benchmark/assessor/acceptance_gate.json` holds the three criteria with
`threshold: null`, and `assessor_agreement.py --gate` prints *"CANNOT EVALUATE:
thresholds are unset… they must be set by the research owner BEFORE adjudication, not
now"*. Given DECISIONS #16, a harness that will happily invent its own bar is the thing
to avoid.

Two bugs surfaced doing this: `case_id` collided across runs (silently dropping one of
37 cases), and rebuilding the label set re-strided the selection, which would have
swapped cases out from under an adjudication in progress. Both fixed and tested.
